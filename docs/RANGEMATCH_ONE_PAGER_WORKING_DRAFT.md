## Buyer journey map (in scope) — plain actions + competitor contrast

**Rule:** Write what buyers **do**, not abstract “story / evidence / data” language.  
Show competitors what we own vs what we do not.

### The real journey (what buyers actually do)

```text
1. Decide the target
   Pick region, budget, and what they want the land for
   (cattle, grazing, farm use, etc.)

2. See what is for sale
   Browse listings, broker packages, photos, tours
   Write down: what is available + what the seller is claiming
   (grass, water, “ready for cattle,” acreage story, etc.)

3. Compare a short list
   Put 2–5 parcels side by side
   Collect public maps / layers / notes and compare
   Ask: which ones still look worth real money and time?

4. Check the land before spending big
   For the top parcel(s):
   - Pull parcel-specific physical facts (terrain, forage, water, climate, soil)
   - Compare those facts to the seller’s claims
   - Ask professional / domain questions until the picture is clear
   Decide: go deeper (site visit + specialists) or stop / deprioritize

5. Only then: field + specialists
   Hire eyes on the ground and experts for what still must be verified live
```

**Stop here for our product.**  
Legal, contract, close, operate = after they already chose to pursue.

---

### Where money gets wasted

| Step | Cheap / expensive | Common failure |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| 1–3 Target → compare | Relatively cheap | Believe listing photos and broker claims too fast |
| **4 Check the land before spending big** | **Should be cheap desk work** | **Skipped or done with generic ChatGPT + random maps** |
| 5 Field + specialists | Expensive | Paid too early, because step 4 never really happened |

**Draft paragraph (v3 — send this back to DVT)**

> Ranch and farmland buyers do not lack a buying process. They define what they want, review what is for sale, compare a short list, and then hit the hard question: **is this land credible enough to justify field review, specialists, and deeper spend right now?**  
> Answering that question requires collecting a lot of information — broker claims, listing photos, maps, satellite imagery, soil layers, water indicators, terrain and climate context, and local assumptions — and turning it into a judgment about what the land can actually support.  
> Today, that early check means jumping across different websites one by one and stitching the pieces together. Ask ChatGPT, and it still cannot answer: it has language, not this parcel’s physical-world facts. The work is slow, easy to misread, and experience-dependent. When the early read is wrong, the cost compounds later in travel, specialists, and diligence that should never have been spent.
